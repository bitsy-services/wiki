ERC-1155 is a multi-token standard: one contract holds many token IDs, and each
ID carries its own supply. An ID with a supply of one behaves like an
[ERC-721](/wiki/economics/finance/defi/ethereum/erc-721) token; an ID with a
supply of a million behaves like an
[ERC-20](/wiki/economics/finance/defi/ethereum/erc-20). The standard does not
make you choose up front.

## What it buys you

- **One deployment for a whole catalogue.** A game with a thousand item types
  deploys one contract rather than a thousand.
- **Batch operations.** `safeBatchTransferFrom` moves several IDs in a single
  call, which is the main gas argument for the standard.
- **Both token shapes at once.** Fungible currency and
  [non-fungible](/wiki/economics/finance/defi/nft) items can live in the same
  contract and be transferred together.

The balance mapping is two-dimensional — `balanceOf(account, id)` — which is
the structural difference from both of the standards it merges.

As with [ERC-721](/wiki/economics/finance/defi/ethereum/erc-721), ERC-165
interface detection is mandatory: a compliant contract answers
`supportsInterface(0xd9b67a26)` with true, and that is how callers tell what
they are dealing with.

## The tradeoff

Tooling support is thinner than for ERC-721, and the flexibility is not free:
because an ID's supply is a runtime fact rather than a property of the
interface, a consumer cannot tell from the application binary interface
whether it is looking at something unique. The base standard does not even
define a supply query — `totalSupply` comes from OpenZeppelin's optional
`ERC1155Supply` extension, and indexers without it reconstruct supply from
`TransferSingle` and `TransferBatch` events. They do not all do it the same way.

Approvals are collection-wide only. There is no per-ID `approve`, just
`setApprovalForAll` — so the approval-scope caution on the
[NFT](/wiki/economics/finance/defi/nft) page applies here with no finer-grained
alternative.

The stranding pitfall on that page does *not* carry over. ERC-1155 has no
unsafe transfer variant: `safeTransferFrom` and `safeBatchTransferFrom` are the
only ways to move a token, and both invoke `onERC1155Received` or
`onERC1155BatchReceived` on a contract recipient. The mistake ERC-721 lets you
make is simply not expressible here.

Metadata comes from `uri(uint256)`, and the detail implementers most often miss
is that the returned string may contain the literal token `{id}`, which clients
substitute with the ID as **lowercase hex, zero-padded to 64 characters, with
no `0x` prefix** — not the decimal form, and not the prefixed form that every
other tool hands you. One URI can then serve a whole catalogue.
