How value gets created, priced, and moved. Four sections sit beneath this one, and they divide finance in three: how the machinery works, what the law permits it to do, and how both get abused.

[DeFi](/wiki/economics/defi) is the largest section of the wiki, where the primitives — [currency](/wiki/economics/defi/currency), [lending, market making](/wiki/economics/defi/amm), and [options](/wiki/economics/defi/options) — are rebuilt on public blockchains, with a [smart contract](/wiki/economics/defi/smart-contract) in place of a clearing house.

[Payments](/wiki/economics/payments) is the machinery the rebuild is measured against — the rails that already move a trillion dollars a year between ordinary American bank accounts, and [Zelle](/wiki/economics/payments/zelle) in particular, which arrived at irrevocable instant settlement by a completely different route and inherited a recognisably similar set of problems.

[Regulation](/wiki/economics/regulation) is the constraint both run into. It covers US financial regulation from the builder's side: the [Bank Secrecy Act](/wiki/economics/regulation/bank-secrecy-act) and the [anti-money-laundering](/wiki/economics/regulation/anti-money-laundering) regime built on it, who counts as a [money services business](/wiki/economics/regulation/money-services-business), and where rules written for correspondent banking stop fitting a permissionless ledger.

[Fraud](/wiki/economics/fraud) is what the other three produce between them. Irreversible settlement, permissionless issuance, and identity that exists only at the edges combine into a set of frauds that are individually old — [Ponzi schemes](/wiki/economics/fraud/ponzi-scheme), [pump and dump](/wiki/economics/fraud/pump-and-dump), [wash trading](/wiki/economics/fraud/wash-trading) — and collectively cost US victims a reported $9.3 billion in 2024. The section is organised by mechanism rather than by loss: what the fraud does to the token, the offering, the holder, or the proceeds.

All four are finance. The section is scoped wider than that — monetary systems, incentives and mechanism design, and the economics of running computation at scale — and a subject that is economics without being finance gets a sibling to these four rather than a home inside one of them. Two neighbours hold material a reader might look for here: the cryptography a chain is built out of is [Computer Science](/wiki/cs) — [content addressing](/wiki/cs/ipfs), [zero-knowledge proofs](/wiki/cs/zero-knowledge-proofs) — and keeping a deploy key off disk is [Security](/wiki/security).
