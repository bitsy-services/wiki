Every page in the DeFi section assumes a chain underneath it, and most assume [Ethereum](/wiki/economics/defi/chains/ethereum). This section holds the specific chains and storage networks the wiki covers — a section or a page each — and the property each was chosen for. What a chain *is* in general, the ledger and the accounts and the transactions, is [Blockchain](/wiki/economics/defi/blockchain); this section is the instances.

[Ethereum](/wiki/economics/defi/chains/ethereum) is the reference implementation of a programmable blockchain and the platform the rest of the section assumes. Its own section covers the improvement-proposal process and the token standards that came out of it, from the fungible-token interface every wallet reads to the vault and agent standards built on top. The others here were each chosen for one property: [Sui](/wiki/economics/defi/chains/sui) for object-centric throughput, [IOTA](/wiki/economics/defi/chains/iota) for machine-to-machine transfer, feeless in its original design, and [Sapphire](/wiki/economics/defi/chains/sapphire) for a confidential Ethereum-compatible chain where contract state and calldata are encrypted inside a hardware enclave.

[Arweave](/wiki/economics/defi/chains/arweave) and [Filecoin](/wiki/economics/defi/chains/filecoin) are two answers to decentralized storage. Arweave prices it as an endowment — pay once, store forever. Filecoin prices it as a dated contract with recurring proofs that the bytes are still there. The addressing scheme both build on is [content addressing](/wiki/cs/ipfs), which sits under Computer Science because the durable idea is the address, not the storage market.
